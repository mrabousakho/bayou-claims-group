# DRAFT — Claimant Outreach Letter (DO NOT SEND — attorney review required)

This template is a placeholder for the *shape* of the letter, not ready-to-send copy. Per the
compliance memo, whether and how you can contact a claimant — and what you can offer them —
depends entirely on which business model (attorney-led, data/software, or general
unclaimed-property finder) you land on. Do not use this until a Texas attorney has confirmed the
model and reviewed this exact text.

---

Dear [OWNER NAME],

Our records research indicates that Harris County may be holding excess proceeds of
approximately $[AMOUNT] related to a tax foreclosure sale of property at [ADDRESS], case number
[CAUSE NO.], sold on [DATE].

[MODEL-DEPENDENT PARAGRAPH — one of:]
- *(Data model)* We can provide you with the complete case research we've compiled — cause
  number, filing deadline, and documentation checklist — for a flat fee of $[X], whether or not
  you ultimately recover funds. You are free to file the petition yourself or hire any attorney
  you choose.
- *(Attorney-led model)* Our affiliated attorney, [NAME], State Bar No. [X], can review your case
  and, if you choose to retain them, file the petition on a contingency fee capped by Texas law
  at 25% of the amount recovered or $1,000, whichever is less.

You should know:
- Texas law requires the county to hold these funds for two years from the sale date; the
  deadline to file a claim in this case is [DATE].
- You are never required to pay anyone to help you recover funds that are legally yours — you can
  file the petition yourself at no cost beyond court filing fees, using the Harris County Law
  Library (1019 Congress, Houston) for self-help resources.

[CLOSING / CONTACT INFO]

---

### Compliance flags for attorney review
- Confirm timing restrictions on first contact (see compliance memo).
- Confirm required disclosures under whichever statute governs the final fee model.
- Confirm this doesn't read as debt-collection-style or high-pressure solicitation — claimants
  here are often in financial distress.
